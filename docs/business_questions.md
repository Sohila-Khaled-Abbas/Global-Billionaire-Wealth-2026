# Core Business & Analytical Questions

This analytical project investigates global wealth concentration, capital accumulation, and macroeconomic exposure across 80 sovereign nations and 3,384 billionaires in 2026. Below are the key research questions addressed by the dataset, data model, and Power BI reporting platform.

---

## 1. Macroeconomic Exposure & Wealth Concentration

### Q1: Which nations exhibit the highest vulnerability to billionaire wealth concentration relative to domestic output?
- **Analytical Objective**: Compare cumulative billionaire wealth against annual nominal GDP across all 80 countries.
- **Key Metric**: `[Billionaire Wealth to GDP %] = DIVIDE(SUM(billionaire_country_aggregate_2026[total_wealth_usd]), AVERAGE(billionaire_country_aggregate_2026[gdp_usd]))`
- **Key Findings**:
  - Small jurisdictions and financial hubs show ratios exceeding 100%: St. Kitts and Nevis (860.7%), Eswatini (362.6%), Cyprus (148.7%), Belize (114.8%), and Hong Kong SAR (103.9%).
  - Major global powers also show high concentration: Russia (30.1%), United States (29.0%), and India (25.3%).
  - Contrastingly, large diversified economies maintain lower ratios: Japan (5.4%), United Kingdom (7.3%), and South Korea (8.7%).

### Q2: How is billionaire density distributed across global populations?
- **Analytical Objective**: Determine the count of billionaires per million citizens to evaluate per-capita wealth generation or relocation effects.
- **Key Metric**: `[Billionaires Per Million Population] = DIVIDE(SUM(billionaire_country_aggregate_2026[billionaire_count]), AVERAGE(billionaire_country_aggregate_2026[population]) / 1000000)`
- **Key Findings**:
  - High density in global wealth sanctuaries: Hong Kong (9.3 per M), Singapore (8.9 per M), Switzerland (4.8 per M), and Cyprus (6.8 per M).
  - High density in large industrialized states: United States (2.88 per M), Germany (2.54 per M), and Canada (1.96 per M).
  - Low per-capita density in massive developing states: India (0.16 per M) and China (0.37 per M), despite massive total billionaire headcounts.

## 2. Sectoral & Industrial Leadership

### Q3: Which economic sectors create the greatest total wealth and largest individual fortunes?
- **Analytical Objective**: Aggregate individual net worth by primary industry sector.
- **Key Metric**: Total wealth ($ USD M) and average wealth per individual by sector.
- **Key Findings**:
  - **Technology** leads all sectors by a wide margin with **$4.75 Trillion USD** across 468 billionaires (average: $10.16B).
  - **Finance & Investments** hosts the largest headcount with 513 billionaires accumulating **$3.17 Trillion USD** (average: $6.17B).
  - **Fashion & Retail** ranks third with **$2.24 Trillion USD** across 301 billionaires, driven by massive mega-fortunes (LVMH, Inditex/Zara, Walmart).
  - Heavy industry & capital goods (**Manufacturing** with 455 billionaires and $1.48 Trillion USD) produces many billionaires with smaller individual average fortunes ($3.25B).

### Q4: Which nations are hyper-dependent on a single dominant wealth sector?
- **Analytical Objective**: Measure the `sector_dominance_score` (% of national billionaire wealth generated by the primary sector).
- **Key Findings**:
  - Spain displays 44.35% dominance by Fashion & Retail (Amancio Ortega / Inditex).
  - France displays 39.58% dominance by Fashion & Retail (LVMH, L'Oréal, Hermès).
  - United States displays 23.35% dominance by Technology (Tesla, Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Nvidia, Oracle).

## 3. Demographics, Origin of Wealth, and Social Mobility

### Q5: What proportion of billionaire fortunes are self-made versus inherited across different geographic regimes?
- **Analytical Objective**: Evaluate the `self_made` flag distribution by country and industry.
- **Key Findings**:
  - Globally, **66.6%** (2,255 individuals) are self-made, while **33.4%** (1,129 individuals) inherited their fortunes.
  - China exhibits the highest self-made ratio among major nations at **97.2%** (513 of 528 billionaires), reflecting rapid post-1980 industrialization.
  - Russia displays **94.0%** self-made classification (140 of 149), originating from post-Soviet privatizations and commodity enterprises.
  - The United States sits at **72.2%** self-made (706 of 978).
  - In contrast, Western European nations show significantly higher inheritance rates: Germany is only **26.9%** self-made (73.1% multi-generational family businesses), Spain is **25.0%** self-made, and France is **46.3%** self-made.

### Q6: What is the gender distribution of extreme wealth, and how does wealth origin differ between genders?
- **Analytical Objective**: Analyze the representation and wealth source of female billionaires compared to male peers.
- **Key Findings**:
  - Severe gender imbalance: **86.0%** Male (2,910 individuals) vs. **14.0%** Female (474 individuals).
  - Among female billionaires, a substantially larger proportion inherited family wealth (e.g., Alice Walton, Francoise Bettencourt Meyers, Julia Koch) compared to male peers who dominate tech and finance entrepreneurship.

### Q7: How does age correlate with net worth across various sectors?
- **Analytical Objective**: Segment billionaire wealth across age bands (<40, 40-59, 60-79, 80+) and compute average age by country and industry.
- **Key Findings**:
  - Global average billionaire age is **66.6 years**.
  - Technology features the lowest average age and fastest wealth compounding velocity (e.g., Mark Zuckerberg at 41, Zhang Yiming at 42).
  - Traditional industrial sectors, real estate, and diversified holding companies skew older (70+ years).

## 4. Single-Individual Concentration & Oligarchy Analysis

### Q8: In which countries does a single billionaire dominate a staggering share of national output or national wealth?
- **Analytical Objective**: Compute `wealth_country_share_pct` and compare the top billionaire's fortune to national GDP.
- **Key Findings**:
  - In Spain, Amancio Ortega accounts for **51.9%** of all Spanish billionaire wealth (8.13% of Spain's entire GDP).
  - In Mexico, Carlos Slim Helù & family represent **48.4%** of Mexican billionaire wealth (6.87% of Mexico's GDP).
  - In France, Bernard Arnault & family represent **25.6%** of French billionaire wealth (4.79% of France's GDP).
  - In the United States, Elon Musk's wealth ($800.6B) equals **9.6%** of total US billionaire wealth and **2.78%** of total US GDP.
